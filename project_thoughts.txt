Feature 3 — Dynamic Conditional Sampling Optimization
Objective
The current conditional sampling model classifies Monday's Open → Close move into three fixed regimes:
Bear: < -1%
Flat: -1% to +1%
Bull: > +1%
These thresholds are currently hardcoded and may not be optimal for every stock.
The goal is to automatically determine the regime boundaries that produce the most accurate historical predictions.
1. Dynamic Threshold Search
Instead of using fixed values, search over multiple possible threshold combinations.
Example search space:
Bear Threshold:
-0.5%
-1.0%
-1.5%
...
-5.0%
Bull Threshold:
+0.5%
+1.0%
+1.5%
...
+5.0%
Each pair of thresholds defines a unique regime classification.

Example:
Bear : < -2.5%
Flat : -2.5% to +3.0%
Bull : > +3.0%

So each stock would have a dynamic range of what's considered a Bear/Flat/Bull market.

Hidden Markov Models (Future Enhancement)

The current implementation assumes the market state is directly observable from recent price transitions.

A future enhancement is to replace the explicit state definition with a Hidden Markov Model (HMM).

In this approach:

the true market regime is hidden
observable market variables (returns, volatility, volume, etc.) are emitted from that hidden state
the HMM estimates the probability of the current hidden regime
Monte Carlo simulations sample conditioned on the inferred hidden regime

This allows the model to recognize latent market environments that are not obvious from price action alone.



9)
Regime-Switching Models (Future Enhancement)

Another future enhancement is to allow transition probabilities to vary based on broader market conditions.

Instead of using a single transition matrix:

Bull → Bull = 55%

the transition matrix may depend on macroeconomic variables such as:

VIX regime
Federal Reserve policy
SPY trend
Market volatility
Earnings season

This creates a regime-switching model in which market behavior changes depending on the overall environment rather than assuming a stationary process.
